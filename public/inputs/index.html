Date, time and colour pickers drawn by a canvas

<input type="date">, type="time" and type="color" open pickers that the browser draws. A page cannot style them, and unlike a customisable select they have no part a canvas can go in. This page tests two things. First, a replacement picker in which every day, hour and swatch is a real button drawn by a canvas, on the same input element. Second, what happens to the built-in controls and pickers when the input itself is drawn in a canvas.

Three pickers made of buttons in a canvas

Tap a field. The time field opens a clock: choose an hour, the hours fold into the centre and the minutes come out. Drag round the face for single minutes. The date field opens a month: the arrows, a sideways swipe or Page Up and Page Down slide to another month. The colour field shows the colours of its <datalist> in rings. On a keyboard, type in the field as usual, or press Alt and Arrow Down to move into the picker.

Findings

  • The built-in date, time and colour pickers have no element. There is no ::picker() for an input, appearance: base-select does nothing on one, and nothing can be put inside an <input>. A canvas can draw the control but not its picker.
  • Built-in controls draw and work in a canvas. All thirteen kinds in the form above are drawn by one drawElementImage() under a rotation. Focus, typing in date segments, the range thumb and the checkbox follow the drawing with no code.
  • A built-in picker opens upright at the bounding box. The colour chooser opens next to the bounding box of the rotated swatch and is not in the canvas's picture. This is the same as the picker of a select in a canvas.
  • A replacement picker can be made of real buttons. In the three pickers at the top each hour, day and swatch is a <button> that is a child of a canvas in a popover. They keep focus, :hover, :focus-visible, aria-pressed and their accessible names while the canvas moves, scales and fades them. A button with no opacity is not drawn, and its hit box is moved away with updateElementGeometry().
  • The canvas draws what is not a control. The clock face, the hand and the disc under the chosen day are 2D drawing in the same canvas, under the buttons. They animate between values, which CSS on the buttons alone cannot do.
  • Stopping the browser's picker. With a mouse, the date and time pickers open only from the small icon in the field, which the library hides with ::-webkit-calendar-picker-indicator. For a touch, the library makes the input readonly while its own picker is open, because a read-only date or time input opens no picker. For type="color" it cancels the click event.
  • Not verified on real hardware. These results are from Chromium 157 driven by Playwright with a mouse and emulated touch. That browser has no window, so the built-in date and time pickers were never seen, and whether the readonly step stops the Android date and time dialogs has not been tried on a phone.

More to try with inputs

  • A range input bent round a dial. Draw the input in thin slices along an arc, and map the pointer's angle back to a value. The hit box is a rectangle, so the pointer mapping has to be written by hand.
  • A date range on one calendar. Two <input type="date"> elements share one canvas calendar, and the canvas draws the band between the two chosen days.
  • A colour wheel that reads its own pixels. Draw a gradient in the popover canvas and read back the pixel under the pointer, with the datalist swatches drawn on top of it at the positions of their colours.
  • A week picker on a year view. 53 week buttons drawn small as a year, with the weeks near the pointer magnified as in the fisheye select.
  • A file input that shows the files. After a choice, draw thumbnails of the chosen files as a fan of cards next to the drawn input.
  • The controls of a <video> or <audio> element. These are built-in controls too. See /select/ and the media experiments in the menu for related work.
