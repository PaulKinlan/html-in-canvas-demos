Inline text elements: sub, bdo, abbr, q, var and br

Six ideas from the element catalogue about elements that sit inside a line of text. None of them is a drawable element: each is part of the picture of the paragraph around it. The question is how much a canvas can do with them from their rectangles alone, and what it can do for the one that has no width, <br>.

Subscripts that settle

Chemical formulas and one equation. The line is drawn with a hole at every <sub> and <sup>. Each subscript then drops into its hole from above and each superscript rises into it from below, one after another.

Water is H2O and glucose is C6H12O6.

CO2 + H2O gives H2CO3.

E = mc2, a2 + b2 = c2. More

A <sub> is not drawable by itself, but it has a box. Clipping a second draw of the paragraph to that box gives the same thing as a picture of the subscript, as long as nothing else is inside the box.

Bidirectional text explained

Three lines. The small numbers over a run are the positions of its characters in the source text, and the arrow goes from the first character to the last. In the Hebrew word and in the run that <bdo dir="rtl"> turns round, the numbers count down along the line.

Left to right

שלום עולם is hello world

Forced the other way

The numbers and arrows are canvas drawing, not text. Each is placed from a Range rectangle of one character, and those rectangles are reported at the place where the sheet was last drawn.

Abbreviations expanded in place

Tap an abbreviation with a dotted line under it. The paragraph is cut under that line, the lower piece moves down, and the abbreviation's title is drawn in the gap. Tap it again, or tap another.

A page is written in HTML and styled with CSS. A script reaches it through the DOM, which is one API among many. Pictures can be SVG.

The browser's own tooltip for title is not in the element's picture and does not appear on a touch screen. Once the paragraph is drawn in two pieces at two places it has no single hit region that fits, so the canvas finds the tapped abbreviation from the tap's position and the boxes of the abbreviations.

Quotation marks as ornaments

Two <q> elements and a <cite>. The canvas draws a band behind each line of a quotation, a large mark where it starts and where it ends, and a wave under the title of the work. The text itself is unchanged and can be selected.

The tide table says high water at four, and again a little before five in the afternoon, which the harbour master reads as stay in. Both lines are from Notes for Small Boats.

A <q> has a rectangle for the opening mark the browser adds, one for each line of its text, and one for the closing mark. The large marks are centred on the first and the last of them.

Variables that light up together

A sentence and the code that goes with it. Tap a variable, or move a mouse over one: every <var> with the same name is outlined, in the sentence and in the code, and a dashed line joins them in order.

Add n numbers into sum, then divide sum by n to get mean.

let sum = 0;
for (const x of list) sum += x;
const n = list.length;
const mean = sum / n;

The tap is an ordinary click on the <var>: the sheet is drawn flat, so its hit region fits. Only the outlines and the line are canvas drawing.

Line breaks as cut lines

A note with a <br> at the end of each line. The dashed lines are where the canvas will cut. Press the button: the note folds along them like a paper fan, and unfolds again.

Back at six.
Soup is on the stove.
Feed the cat, not the dog.
The key is under the pot.
Do not wait up.
Love, M.

The cut lines are not taken from the <br> elements, which are 0 px wide. They are the tops of the text between two breaks, read with a Range while the note is flat.

How it works

  • One drawable block per canvas. The inline elements are not drawable. A piece of the block is drawn by clipping a draw of the whole block to a box.
  • Two kinds of box. offsetLeft, offsetTop, offsetWidth and offsetHeight are layout numbers and do not change with how the block is drawn. The demos that move pieces use them. getClientRects() of an element or a Range gives one rectangle per line, at the place where the block was last drawn. The demos that draw the block flat use those.
  • Frames. A demo asks for frames only while something moves. The demos that read client rectangles draw their first frames twice, because the rectangles follow the draw before.

Findings

Measured in headless Chromium 157 on 2026-11-21. Not yet run on a phone.

  • Inline elements can be treated as pictures by clipping. sub, sup, abbr, q and var have boxes inside a drawable block (a sub was 7 by 15 px in the check), and a draw of the block clipped to one box moves that element alone. Twelve subscripts and superscripts were dropped into place this way.
  • There are two kinds of box, and they behave differently. Client rectangles follow the draw: the rectangle of a var moved by 60 and 30 px when its block was drawn at (60, 30). offsetLeft and offsetTop stayed at 35 and 3. A demo that moves pieces uses the offset numbers. A demo that draws the block flat can use either.
  • A block drawn in two pieces reports rectangles for the last piece only. With the abbreviation's paragraph cut and its lower piece drawn 42 px down, every client rectangle in the paragraph was 42 px down, the ones above the cut too. The canvas finds the tapped abbreviation from the tap's position and the offset boxes.
  • A br has one client rectangle, 0 px wide. A wbr has none. The fold lines come from a Range over the text between two breaks: tops 24 px apart for a 24 px line height.
  • A q has more rectangles than lines. One for the opening mark the browser adds, one for each line of text and one for the closing mark: 3 for a one-line q, 5 for one that wraps once, 8 for the two in the demo.
  • Character rectangles show visual order. In <bdo dir="rtl"> the last of six letters was 35.5 px to the left of the first. In a plain span it was 37.3 px to the right. The Hebrew run of 9 characters and the forced run of 20 were both reported right to left.
  • The title of an abbr is not in the picture. The block was 220 by 24 px with and without it. The demo draws the title from a real element in a gap it opens in the paragraph, and the abbreviations answer to touch and to Enter.
  • Not verified: everything on a phone. Whether Android shows the title tooltip on a long press over a drawn abbreviation, text selection across the ornaments by touch, and Arabic or mixed-direction runs, which this page does not have.
