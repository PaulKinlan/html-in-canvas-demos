Takeaways

  • ElementImage and OffscreenCanvas both transfer across origins with window.postMessage and MessagePorts.
  • A cross-origin document can render your DOM if you give it both your canvas (as an OffscreenCanvas) and the snapshots. The pair must match: the snapshot only draws into the canvas it was captured from.
  • That is a real trust decision. The receiving origin can call getImageData() on what it drew, so lending a snapshot means lending the pixels of that element. Only content that is already "read-back allowed" is in there, but it is your content.
  • It can't go the other way directly. A cross-origin iframe placed inside a drawable canvas is painted blank (same-origin iframes paint). To show another origin's DOM, that origin has to cooperate and send pixels, as in part A.
  • Hit testing survives. When the other origin draws our snapshot, our element's geometry is updated here, so clicks land on the real button.
