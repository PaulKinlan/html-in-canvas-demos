How it works

  • The text element fills the canvas. Its font size is a fraction of canvas.clientWidth, so a narrower canvas wraps the text again. The browser does the wrapping.
  • The text is styled white on a clear background. In the shader, coverage is the smallest of the red, green and blue channels of the premultiplied text texture. A white glyph pixel gives 1, a clear pixel gives 0, and an antialiased edge gives its alpha.
  • A pixel that has alpha but is not white is not a glyph. That is the selection highlight, the caret, the focus ring or a colour emoji. The shader draws those over the effect in their own colour at 60% strength.
  • The video is not a child of the canvas. texImage2D(video), copyExternalImageToTexture() or drawImage(video) reads its current frame. requestVideoFrameCallback() asks for one paint per video frame. Typing, the caret and selection changes raise paint with no request.
  • WebGL and WebGPU do not tell the browser where the element is drawn. The page calls updateElementGeometry() with an identity matrix after each resize, so taps and the caret go to the text as it is laid out.
  • The 2D route has no shader. It draws the text, then draws the video with globalCompositeOperation = 'source-in' (fill) or 'source-out' (punch). It builds those two effects only.

Findings

From Chromium 157 headless with software rendering and software video decoding, 2026-11-21. The rows above say what your browser does.

  • Works: editable DOM text as a shader input next to a video frame. The text stays editable, selectable and wrapped by the browser in every route. Six effects are built.
  • Works with CSS and no canvas: fill and punch. mix-blend-mode: multiply or screen on text over a video gives both, with editable HTML text. An SVG <clipPath> or <mask> with <text> gives the fill for SVG text only.
  • Works: the CSS blend inside a drawable element. When the video and the blended text are both inside the drawn element, the blend is in its picture.
  • Limit: the blend does not reach the canvas. mix-blend-mode on a drawn element does not blend with pixels the canvas already holds. globalCompositeOperation does that job in a 2D canvas.
  • Limit: background-clip: text cannot use a video. Chromium has no element() and no -webkit-canvas() image. A paint() worklet exists but cannot be given a video frame.
  • Needs the canvas: displace, chrome, mosaic and weight. Each one reads the text's pixels and the video's pixels at different places and computes a new pixel. CSS blending and clipping combine the two pictures at the same place only. The 2D canvas route is in the same position without a per-pixel loop in script.
  • Selection and caret are in the text texture. A route that uses alpha as coverage (2D source-in) turns the selection highlight into a block of video. The shader keys on white, so it can tell glyphs from the highlight.
  • Not verified here: the WebGPU route on screen, a hardware-decoded frame through texImage2D(video) on Android, touch selection handles and the on-screen keyboard on the drawn text, and the frame rate on a phone.
