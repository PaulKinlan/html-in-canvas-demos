1 · Slotting DOM into a canvas

HTML-in-canvas wants the drawn DOM to be a descendant of the <canvas>. A shadow root lets the canvas live in the shadow tree while your content stays in the light DOM: each named <slot> inside the canvas pulls in the matching child, and that child is drawn with drawElementImage(). Takeaways

  • Slotted light-DOM elements can be drawn directly. drawElementImage(lightChild) works when the light child is assigned to a slot inside the canvas, because layout and paint use the flat tree.
  • The <slot> itself can't be the drawable. It is display: contents, so it has no box and no paint record ("No cached paint record for element"). Put drawable on the slotted element, or on a real wrapper element in the shadow tree around the slot.
  • A shadow wrapper works too. <div drawable><slot></slot></div> inside the canvas draws everything slotted into it as one image, which is handy when you don't want to touch the author's markup.
  • The limit is the host's children. A slot can only reproject children of its shadow host. It does not let you pull an arbitrary element from elsewhere in the page into the canvas; for that, see the OffscreenCanvas and cross-tab demos.
  • Hit testing, focus and selection follow the drawn position, because drawElementImage updates the element's geometry automatically.
