Focus trail and heading outline drawn by a canvas

Two accessibility views from the element catalogue, and two more on the same form. A canvas draws a form and, when focus moves, a ring that travels from the control that lost focus to the one that gained it. The same canvas can mark the size of each touch target and draw a line from each label to its control. A second canvas draws an article with a list of its headings beside it. Each row of that list is the heading's own pixels, drawn a second time.

Focus ring that leads the eye

Tap a field or a button in the form, press Tab, or use "Next stop". The ring flies from the old position to the new one and the rest of the form is shaded. "Target sizes" and "Label lines" draw two more views over the same form.

The canvas learns that focus moved from focusin, which names both elements. It reads where each one was drawn from getBoundingClientRect(). When the ring arrives, the canvas draws that rectangle of the form's picture a second time, 12% larger, for a third of a second. The form has four faults on purpose: a 20 px button, a field with only a placeholder, a label whose for matches nothing, and the field that label was meant for.

Heading outline beside the page

Scroll the article with a finger or the wheel. The list on the right has one row for each heading. Tap a row to go to that heading. Change a heading with the controls under the picture and its row changes, because the row is drawn from the article's picture.

Heading levels in a document

This article is a child of the canvas. The list beside it is drawn by the canvas from the headings below.

What a level means

The elements h1 to h6 give a heading a level from 1 to 6. A heading starts a section. The section ends at the next heading of the same level or of a level with a lower number.

Nesting

An h3 that follows an h2 starts a part of the h2 section. The outline indents it and draws a line to its parent.

Skipped levels

A heading two or more levels below the one before it leaves a gap in the outline. The control under the picture changes this heading to an h5. The outline then marks its row in red. See Listing every heading.

How a screen reader uses headings

Headings are a way to move through a page without reading all of it.

Moving by heading

A screen reader can move from one heading to the next. TalkBack on Android does this when its reading control is set to "Headings".

Listing every heading

Some screen readers show all headings of the page in a list, with their levels. The rail beside this article is the same list, drawn. Back to Skipped levels.

Elements that count

The outline collects h1 to h6 and every element with role="heading".

A div with role="heading"

The line above is a div. Its level comes from aria-level. With no aria-level the level is 2.

What the outline leaves out

A heading that is not rendered, or is inside aria-hidden="true" or inert, is left out. Shadow trees and frames are not followed.

    The rows are not text drawn by the canvas. Each one is drawElementImage(article, sx, sy, sw, sh, dx, dy, dw, dh) with the rectangle of one heading, so the font, the wrapping and any edit are the article's. The links that take the taps are children of the canvas drawn with globalAlpha = 0. Scrolling is the browser's own: the canvas is position: sticky inside a scroll container and draws the article at minus scrollTop.

    How it works

    • Each picture is one 2D canvas with content="drawable". Its children are ordinary HTML and the paint handler draws them with drawElementImage(). Everything else in the picture (ring, tail, shade, target boxes, label lines, the rail) is drawn after them in the same context.
    • Focus: focusin bubbles to the canvas. event.target is the element that gained focus and event.relatedTarget the one that lost it. When the old element is outside the canvas, the ring starts at the nearest point of the canvas edge. The flight lasts 220 to 560 ms, longer for a longer move. With prefers-reduced-motion the ring is drawn at the new position at once and a straight line with an arrow head shows where it came from.
    • Positions: getBoundingClientRect() of a canvas child, or of an element inside one, reports where it was last drawn. "Drawn at 60%" draws the form smaller, and the ring, the target boxes and the label lines follow without any code for it.
    • Target sizes: every link, button and form control is measured as drawn. A side under 24 CSS px is red, under 48 px amber. 24 is the size in WCAG 2.2 "Target Size (Minimum)". 48 is the size Android asks for. The view measures the control's own box. It does not add the label of a checkbox, which also takes the tap.
    • Label lines: a line runs from the words of each <label> to its control, found through control.labels. The words are measured with a Range. A control named only by aria-label has a dashed outline and the name on a plate, because nothing visible names it. A control with only a placeholder is amber, a control with nothing is red, and a label whose for matches no control is marked.
    • Heading outline: the headings are collected from the DOM with a rule of about 15 lines. A MutationObserver on the article runs it again after an edit. The rail's rows are rectangles of the article's picture. The article is drawn last and whole, which puts its hit box back after the pieces.
    • The article is taller than the canvas. The canvas is position: sticky in a scroll container, with a spacer of the article's height after it. A scroll listener asks for a paint, and the handler draws the article at minus scrollTop. Tapping a row calls scrollTo() on the container and focus() on the heading, so the ring flies from the row to the heading.
    • Without the API, or after a lost context, both canvases are replaced by their children as plain HTML. The outline is then an ordinary list of links.

    Findings

    From Chromium 157, headless, on 2026-11-21. The rows above say what your browser does. Real key presses and touch events were sent by tools/a11y-views/check.mjs.

    • Works: a focus ring is in the picture when the focused element is inside the drawable element. A 4 px red outline round a button inside a drawable div was found in the canvas pixels at the button's drawn position. Focus moving raised one paint event with the div in changedElements.
    • Limit: the ring is cut at the border box of the drawable element. A button in the corner of its drawable parent kept only the part of its ring that was inside the parent. An outline round the drawable element itself is not in its picture at all, unless outline-offset is negative.
    • Found: no pixel change, no paint event. With outline: none on the focused element, moving focus raised no paint event. A trail that waits for paint would not start. focusin fired every time, with relatedTarget set.
    • Works: the trail follows the picture. The start and the end of the trail were at the drawn positions of the two buttons when the fixture was drawn at half size and offset.
    • Found: a control drawn smaller is a smaller target. A 40 px button drawn at half size took a hit only inside its 20 px drawn box. The target view reported 20 by 20.
    • Works: label text measured with a Range follows the picture at half size, so the line starts at the drawn words.
    • Works: a row of the outline is the heading's pixels. A piece draw of the article with the heading's rectangle put the heading's colour at the row. After the pieces, one whole draw of the article put its hit box back: a point on a link in the article returned the link.
    • Works: links drawn with globalAlpha = 0 take the tap. elementFromPoint() at a row returned the link for that row, and the canvas pixels there came only from the canvas's own drawing.
    • Found: scrolling the container raises no paint event. The page asks for one from a scroll listener. Changing a heading's text raises one. Changing aria-level raises none, so the outline is rebuilt from a MutationObserver.
    • Works: the Tab key scrolls the article by itself. Tab from above the canvas reached a link about 710 px down the article, below the 520 px canvas. The browser scrolled the container to 480 px, which put the link in the middle of the canvas. The page did not move. The page has no code for this.
    • Works: a finger drag on the drawn article scrolls the container. A 250 px drag moved scrollTop by 235 px and the page by 0 px.
    • Not verified here: a phone and a screen reader. TalkBack moves its own accessibility focus, which is not DOM focus. Whether focusin fires when TalkBack moves to a control, and so whether the trail runs, was not measured. The line under the first picture counts focusin events: move through the form with TalkBack and read the count. The frame rate of the article scrolling under a finger was not measured on a phone either.
