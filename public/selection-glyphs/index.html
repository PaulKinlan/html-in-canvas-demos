Selected letters as particles

Select some of the text. When the selection stops changing, its letters come loose and fall to the bottom of the article. “Swirl” moves them in rings and “Put them back” returns each one to its place. The text in the DOM does not change at any point.

Loose type

A page of metal type was a few thousand separate pieces held in a frame by pressure. If the frame was dropped, the letters fell out and had to be sorted by hand before anything could be printed again.

Text on a screen has no pieces. A selection can still be measured one character at a time, and a canvas that draws the text can leave any character out and draw it somewhere else.

How it works

A Range around a single character has one client rectangle. The page collects those rectangles for the selection and gives each one a particle. On every frame the canvas draws the article through a clip path that leaves out the home rectangle of every loose letter, then draws each letter at its particle's position and angle. A letter comes from one of two places. The snapshot is a copy of the canvas taken at the moment the letters are let go, and each letter is a drawImage() of a small part of it. The live route calls drawElementImage() once per letter with a clip the size of the letter, so a letter in the air still follows changes to the element (hover, a new colour, an edit).

Findings

Measured in headless Chromium 157 on Linux with a software renderer, 2026-11-21.

  • One character has one rectangle. A Range from offset i to i + 1 gives the box of that character at its drawn position. The box is the advance width and the line's text height, not the ink, so a tall italic letter can lose a sliver to its neighbour's box.
  • The canvas is transparent where the element has no ink. The article has no background of its own, so a piece of the canvas is a letter with nothing behind it. No colour keying is needed.
  • An even-odd clip removes letters from the element's picture. The text is drawn once, with holes. The DOM text, the selection and copy are unchanged while the letters are away.
  • Both ways of drawing a letter work. The timing check below gives the cost of each on the device that runs it. The live route replays the element's whole paint record once per letter.
  • A resize moves every home rectangle, so the page puts the text back whole when the element's width changes.

Not verified: frame rate on a phone with several hundred letters, and the automatic drop after a touch selection made with the Android handles (the page waits 0.7 s after the last change).
