How it works

  • speechSynthesis.speak() takes a SpeechSynthesisUtterance. While it speaks, the utterance fires boundary events with charIndex, and in newer engines charLength and the name word or sentence.
  • The page makes a Range from charIndex to the end of the word and reads getClientRects(). The rectangles are relative to the article, so the canvas can use them whatever size it draws the article at.
  • The canvas draws the article three times: once at 32% opacity, once clipped to the rectangles of the words already spoken, and once for the current word with the chosen effect. The spotlight's rectangle moves a fraction of the way to the new word on every frame.
  • The lens draws only the word's rectangle, 1.7 times larger. A draw call also moves the element's hit-test region, so after it the paragraph could not be tapped where it appears. The handler ends with one more full-size draw inside an empty clip, which draws nothing and restores the region.
  • The fourth effect draws nothing extra. Two CSS Custom Highlights are set on the same ranges and the browser paints them into the element's picture.
  • A tap calls document.caretPositionFromPoint(). The offset it returns is moved back to the start of the word, and a new utterance is spoken from there. Its charIndex values are then offsets from that point.

Findings

From Chromium 157 headless, 2026-11-21, which has no voices. The rows above and the line under the buttons say what your browser does.

  • Works: a boundary event to a drawn word. With boundary events dispatched by hand, the range, its rectangle and the three canvas effects land on the right word.
  • Works: charLength can be absent. The word's end is then found in the text from charIndex.
  • Found: a partial draw moves the whole hit-test region. After the nine-argument draw of one word, elementFromPoint() at another word answered with the canvas, not the article. A final full-size draw inside an empty clip puts it back and adds no pixels.
  • Works: caretPositionFromPoint() inside a drawn element. It returns the text node and the offset of the word under the point.
  • Works: Custom Highlights are in the picture. A change to a Highlight is drawn after requestPaint().
  • Not run here: real speech. The test browser has no voices and speak() ends with the error synthesis-failed. The page then steps the words by a timer, which is the same path a voice without boundary events takes.
  • Not verified: whether the voices of Chrome 159 on Android fire boundary events, whether they carry charLength, and how far the events lead or trail the sound.
