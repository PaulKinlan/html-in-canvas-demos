How it works

  • The slide is two elements of 640 by 360 layout pixels. The back one has the background and the body. The front one has the title. The presenter is drawn between them or after both.
  • The presenter is drawn from a <video> that is not a child of the canvas. Nothing of it exists for hit testing, so every tap goes to the slide.
  • The clip with an alpha channel is drawn with a 2D context. A camera, the drawn figure and three of the effects need a shader. The page then makes a second canvas with a WebGL 2 context and moves the two slide elements into it.
  • The video does not invalidate the canvas, because it is not in it. requestVideoFrameCallback() calls requestPaint() for each frame.
  • The chroma key compares the chroma of each pixel with a key colour. The difference key compares each pixel with a frame captured when the person was out of the picture.

Findings

From Chromium 157 with software rendering and its fake camera, 2026-11-21. The rows above say what your browser does.

  • Works: the slide takes input under the presenter. A link, a radio button, a range input and a text field were used through the video's pixels, and text under it was selected. This holds on both routes.
  • Works: alpha video by three routes. A <video> child of a drawable element, drawImage(video) and texImage2D(video) all keep the alpha of the VP9 clip.
  • Works: chroma key and difference key in one shader. Both need no browser support beyond texImage2D(video). The difference key needs a still camera and a captured background.
  • Works: the composed canvas as a stream. captureStream() through RTCPeerConnection carries the slide and the presenter. MediaRecorder records it.
  • Limit: a presenter that is a <video> element takes the taps. As a child of the slide it is hit on its clear pixels too. It needs pointer-events: none.
  • Limit: the video raises no paint. A video outside the canvas gave 0 paints a second until the page called requestPaint() for each frame.
  • Needed: preserveDrawingBuffer for the WebGL stream. Without it captureStream() of the WebGL canvas gave black frames, because the canvas is drawn only in its paint handler. The 2D canvas needs nothing.
  • Limit: the bend effect moves pixels, not hit boxes. A control drawn up to 27 pixels from its place is still hit at its place.
  • Not available here: segmentation by the browser. The fake camera lists neither backgroundBlur nor backgroundSegmentationMask in getCapabilities(). The mask route is written and has not run.
  • Not verified here: a real camera, the two keys on a real background, hardware-decoded alpha video, and the frame rate of the shader on a phone. The checks above and the status line under the picture report them on your device.
