Other people's selections

An article drawn by a canvas, with the selections of other readers drawn into it. Two simulated readers select sentences. Select some text yourself, then press “Show a second reader” to get a second copy of the page in a frame: a selection made in either copy appears in the other. A copy open in another tab joins too.

Reading the same page

When two people read one document, each of them can see what the other has selected. A shared editor does this by sending the start and end of the selection, not the pixels, and each reader's own copy of the page draws the mark.

A browser keeps one selection per document, and in Chrome it holds one range. So another reader's selection cannot be a second real selection. It has to be drawn some other way: wrapped in elements, registered as a custom highlight, or painted by a canvas that knows where the text is.

Here the canvas draws them. Each reader has a colour, a bar at the end of the selection that is still moving, and a flag with a name. Your own selection is drawn the same way and stays a real selection that can be copied.

    How it works

    Each copy of the page listens for selectionchange, converts its selection to a start and an end offset in the article's text and posts them on a BroadcastChannel. A copy that receives offsets builds a Range from them, reads getClientRects() for each text node in it, and draws the rectangles in the canvas before the text and the flag after it. The receiving page's DOM and its own selection do not change. Tabs and frames of one origin share the channel. Between two people the same message would go through a server.

    Live checks

    These start when you press the button. They use their own fixtures and their own channel.

    Findings

    Measured in headless Chromium 157 on Linux with a software renderer, 2026-11-21.

    • A range that is not selected still has rectangles. Range.getClientRects() works for any range, so a canvas can draw any number of other readers. The receiving document's selection and DOM are not changed (checked below).
    • Offsets into textContent are enough to send a selection between copies whose text is the same. They survive different line breaks, widths and fonts, because each copy measures its own rectangles. They do not survive an edit of the text.
    • A frame of the same origin is a second reader. Each document has its own selection, so selecting in the frame does not clear the selection in the page, and both are sent.
    • What the canvas adds over ::highlight(): a Custom Highlight could colour another reader's range with no canvas. It cannot draw the name flag, the bar at the moving end or an animation between two positions.

    Not verified: selecting by touch in the page and in the frame on a phone, and two phones through a server (this page has no server, only a channel inside one browser).
