An infinite board of live pages

A zoomable, pannable canvas where every tile is a working document: notes you can type in, a to-do list, a spreadsheet, a sketch pad, and other pages of this site running in iframes. One drawElementImage() per tile under a zoom transform. The text is re-rasterised at every zoom level, and clicks, focus and typing land on the real elements.

Drag the background or scroll to pan. Ctrl + wheel or pinch to zoom. Double-click the background for a new note. Drag a tile by its title bar, resize from its corner. Page tiles wake up when you click them; Escape hands the wheel back to the board.

Takeaways

  • Zoom is free and sharp. drawElementImage() under a canvas transform replays the element's paint record, so text is re-rasterised at 800% rather than stretched. Hit testing follows the same transform with no extra code.
  • Iframes are tiles like any other. A same-origin iframe inside the canvas paints, takes clicks, typing and wheel scrolling at any zoom, and repaints the board when its own content changes. Other HTML-in-canvas pages, a WebGL scene and this board itself all nest.
  • An iframe swallows input. Wheel and pointer events inside a frame never reach the board, so each page tile has a cover that hands it the pointer only after a click, the way a map embed does.
  • Undrawn does not mean unclickable. A tile that is culled or replaced by a placeholder keeps the hit region of its last draw. Park it with updateElementGeometry() or stale tiles catch clicks.
  • Stacking is the canvas's, not CSS's. When tiles overlap, the browser hits the one drawn last. z-index on the elements changes nothing, so bring-to-front is just drawing a tile later, and iframes never have to move in the DOM.
  • Focus can drive the camera. Tab still walks every control on the board in DOM order, so a focusin listener that pans to the focused tile makes the whole surface keyboard-reachable.
  • Semantic zoom is a draw decision. Below a threshold a tile is drawn as a labelled rectangle instead of its DOM. It stays in the document, findable and focusable; focusing it zooms back in.
