HTML-in-canvas experiments

Exploring the experimental HTML-in-canvas API (<canvas content="drawable">, drawElementImage(), captureElementImage(), the paint event): can DOM be reprojected into a canvas without living inside it, and how far can those pixels and snapshots travel? Every page runs live tests in your browser and reports what works and what doesn't.

1 · Slots

A <canvas-slot> element: light-DOM children are slotted into a shadow canvas and drawn, still interactive.

2 · OffscreenCanvas

Transfer ElementImage snapshots to a worker, animate off the main thread, and reproject parts of the DOM.

3 · Cross-tab

Reproject a piece of one page into another tab, with clicks and typing sent back.

4 · Cross-origin

What survives postMessage across origins: ElementImages, transferred OffscreenCanvases, ImageBitmaps.

Building block

All demos use /lib/canvas-slot.js, a small custom element: a slot name plus a canvas.

<script type="module" src="/lib/canvas-slot.js"></script>
<canvas-slot id="c">
  <h2 slot="title">Real DOM</h2>
  <button slot="cta">Still clickable</button>
</canvas-slot>
<script>
  c.onrender = ({ ctx, draw }) => { ctx.rotate(0.05); draw('title', 20, 20); draw('cta', 20, 90); };
</script>

Add worker="./renderer.js" and the canvas is transferred to an OffscreenCanvas in that worker, which receives the slotted elements as ElementImage snapshots.
