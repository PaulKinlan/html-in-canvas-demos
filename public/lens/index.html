A lens over live DOM

Move the pointer over the card. The loupe is not a screenshot: the browser replays the element's paint record at the lens's scale, so the 4px text is re-rasterized sharp, and everything under the glass is still the real page. Click the button through the lens, type in the field, or press Tab and the lens follows focus. On a touch screen, drag a finger across the card: the lens rides above your finger and the page does not scroll (switch the lens off to scroll from the card).

How it works

  • The content is slotted, not moved. Your markup stays a child of <dom-lens>. A shadow root holds a canvas with one drawable wrapper around a <slot>, sized to the content.
  • Magnifier: draw the content once at rest, then again inside a circular clip with a scale about the lens centre. Two drawElementImage() calls per frame.
  • Fisheye: a fisheye is not an affine transform, and drawElementImage() only takes one. So the lens is cut into a polar mesh of triangles and the element is drawn once per triangle, clipped, with the affine transform that maps that triangle's source onto the screen. Neighbouring triangles share edges, so the image is continuous, and each one is re-rasterized, so it is vector-sharp. A shader would be one draw but would magnify a fixed-resolution texture. Any warp can be drawn this way.
  • X-ray: inside the clip the content is drawn faded and inverted, with the live layout boxes of every element on top (interactive ones in amber). It is a devtools overlay you can aim.
  • Hit testing: the pointer is the fixed point of the magnification, so the element under the cursor is the one you see there. For a pinned lens the element's geometry is switched to the magnified transform while the pointer is inside it.
