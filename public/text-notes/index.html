Annotated article: ruby, pull quote, selection glow, kinetic heading, footnotes, strike-through

The article below is one element drawn by a 2D canvas. The canvas draws parts of the article's picture a second time, or leaves parts out, to make six effects. The heading's glyphs fly in. Ruby annotations show only near the pointer or the last tap. The quote is drawn again behind the text, large and faint, and moves as the page scrolls. Selected text glows. A tap on a footnote number slides the note out beside the line. Struck and inserted words get a pen stroke, and with the checkbox on a tap on a word strikes it.

Notes drawn by the canvas

Ruby text is a small annotation above a word. Japanese print uses it for readings: 東京とうきょう is Tokyo, 漢字かんじ are Chinese characters, 振り仮名ふりがな is the annotation itself and 日本語にほんご is the language. Chinese uses pinyin: 北京Běijīng, 上海Shànghǎi.

A canvas that draws an element can draw it twice, at any size.

The canvas element came from Apple in 2004.1 Drawing HTML into one was not possible done with an SVG image added as drawElementImage(), which Chrome has behind a flag.2 Text with this background has the colour of the selection.3

1. Apple added canvas to WebKit in 2004, for Dashboard widgets and Safari.
2. The API is described in the WICG html-in-canvas explainer. Chrome 148 and later have it behind the canvas-draw-element flag.
3. The colour key filter keeps every pixel of that colour, so this text glows whenever a selection does.

How it works

The article is a child of a <canvas content="drawable">. On every paint event the page reads the client rectangles it needs and then draws the layers in this order: background, pull quote, glow, the article, ruby, heading glyphs, pen strokes, the open footnote. The page asks for frames only while something is moving.

Ruby near the pointer
The article is drawn through a clip path that has a hole for the box of every <rt>, so no annotation is drawn. For each rt within 130 px of the pointer the page draws that box of the article again with drawElementImage() and a source rectangle, at an alpha that falls with distance. The rt elements stay in the document.
Pull quote behind the column
The rectangle of the <blockquote> is drawn before the article, scaled up to about three times its size at 10% alpha and turned by 4 degrees. Its horizontal position follows the scroll position of the page.
Selection that glows
The browser paints the selection into the article's picture. With "Colour key filter" the page draws the article once more through an SVG filter set as ctx.filter: a colour matrix keeps pixels near the selection colour, a blur spreads them, and the result is added to the canvas. The filter knows nothing about the selection, so the <mark> with the same colour glows too. With "Range rectangles" the page blurs filled rectangles from Range.getClientRects().
Kinetic heading
While the heading animates, its box is clipped out of the article and each glyph box is drawn as its own piece, with a translation, a rotation and a scale that all end at the glyph's place in the layout. The <h2> is not changed. It keeps its text, its place in the outline and its hit region.
Footnotes that slide out
Each note is an <aside> that is a child of the canvas and is not drawn until its link is tapped. The click on the link is cancelled, and the canvas draws the note under the line (in the margin beside the line on a wide screen), moving in from the right edge. A drawn note has a hit region, so the link inside it works. Without the API the notes are at the foot of the article and the links jump to them.
Strike-through drawn by hand
s, del and ins have text-decoration: none inside the canvas. The canvas draws a pen stroke along each of their line boxes: two passes through struck text, an underline and a caret for inserted text. A new element is drawn in over time. "Tap a word to strike it" wraps the word under the tap in a <del>. A second tap removes it.

Live checks

These run on their own fixtures in the corner of the window, after a tap on the button. Two of them set the document's selection for a moment and put it back.

Findings

  • A 2D canvas filter applies to drawElementImage(), both a blur() function and a url() reference to an SVG filter. A colour matrix can therefore select pixels of the element's picture by colour with no readback.
  • The selection highlight is in the element's picture, and a change of selection fires paint with no call from the page.
  • A source rectangle of an element drawn at five times its size is drawn from the recorded picture at that size. The glyph edges are as sharp as at the original size, unlike a scaled copy of the canvas pixels.
  • An <rt> has its own client rectangle. A clip path with a hole for that rectangle removes the annotation and leaves the base text, because ruby text is laid out in its own box above the line.
  • A drawable child of the canvas has a hit region only in a frame that draws it, and where two children overlap the one drawn last takes the click. A footnote that is not drawn does not take clicks, so a closed note needs no extra step.
  • caretPositionFromPoint() on a drawn element returns the text node and offset under the point, at the drawn position.
  • A draw with a source rectangle moves the element's hit region to the piece and gives it the size of the piece. updateElementGeometry() afterwards moves the region back but keeps the size of the piece: a 120 px heading answered at 6 of 36 points. A whole draw inside an empty clip restores the whole region. The page ends every frame with that draw. The rectangles from getClientRects() move with the piece too, so the page reads them all before the first such draw.
  • After a mouse click inside the article in the test browser, a selection set from script was in getSelection() but not in the picture until a control outside the canvas was clicked. Cause not found.
  • Not verified: Chrome 159 for Android. Long-press selection inside the article, the cost of the filter on a phone GPU, and whether a tap near a ruby word is a usable replacement for hover. The status line under the demo prints the time of the last paint.
