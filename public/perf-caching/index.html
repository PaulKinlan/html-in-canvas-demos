Caching and snapshot churn

I wanted to know whether caching pays off when only a few cards in a 24-card canvas dashboard change each frame. This page pits full drawElementImage() replay against a main-thread captureElementImage() cache, a plain offscreen 2D canvas per card, and a clipped dirty-rect redraw using nine-argument drawElementImage(). Below that, a separate test churns ElementImage snapshots at 60 fps and watches frame time and memory.

Snapshot churn at 60 fps

  

    Taking an ElementImage snapshot on a timer and forgetting to call close() holds GPU memory until garbage collection.
    This test measures the other case: taking snapshots at 60/s (and 240/s at 4 cards per frame) and closing every one either straight away inside the same paint handler or in batches of 8, against a direct drawElementImage() baseline.

How it works

  
    
    
  • All 24 cards are live drawable children of one <canvas content="drawable" layoutsubtree>. Each card has a static header with a working pin button and a lower metric strip whose text, badge and progress bar mutate at the chosen dirty ratio (0, 1, 6 or 24 cards per frame).
    • 
    
  • In the captureElementImage() cache strategy, the paint handler checks e.changedElements alongside its dirty set and closes each replaced ElementImage before calling captureElementImage() for the new one, keeping open handles capped at 24. When a cell finishes or stops, every cached snapshot is closed so window.__gfx.snapshots.open returns to 0.
    • 
    
  • In the plain offscreen 2D canvas strategy, each card gets a plain non-drawable <canvas> (no layoutsubtree and no willReadFrequently). Dirty cards are drawn once with drawElementImage() and copied into their offscreen canvas with drawImage(); clean cards skip drawElementImage() and blit from their offscreen canvas. All 24 offscreen canvases are zeroed and handed to window.__gfx.retire() on teardown.
    • 
    
  • In the clipped dirty-rect strategy, the paint handler skips ctx.reset() after the first frame, sets a clip rect around each dirty card's lower metric strip, and calls nine-argument drawElementImage(el, sx, sy, sw, sh, dx, dy, dw, dh, { preserveElementGeometry: true }) in CSS pixel coordinates.
    • 
    
  • Each cell runs a short warmup and then records requestAnimationFrame p50 and p95 deltas, JS paint-handler duration, Long Animation Frame (long-animation-frame) count and post-main-thread time (presentationTime - (startTime + duration)), open snapshot counts from window.__gfx.snapshots, and estimated canvas memory from window.__gfx.memory().
    • 
    
  • Pressing "Run full sweep" runs all 16 partial-update cells plus all 4 churn modes and posts a compact summary to /api/reports via window.__gfx.report() on deployed origins.
    •

Findings

  
    
    
  • Not yet measured on real hardware or headless Chromium 157. Press "Run full sweep" on a desktop GPU and on Chrome for Android to compare whether main-thread snapshot caching, offscreen 2D canvas blitting or clipped nine-argument dirty-rect draws beat full 24-card drawElementImage() replay at 0%, 5% and 25% dirty ratios.
    • 
    
  • Close before replace matters for the snapshot budget. With 24 cards cached in ElementImage snapshots, capturing a replacement before closing the old one momentarily hits 25 open handles and trips context-guard.js. Closing the replaced snapshot first keeps the live count capped at 24 and teardown returns it to 0.
    • 
    
  • Nine-argument partial redraws need geometry care. Earlier experiments (/input-ideas/, /select-selection/) found that nine-argument drawElementImage() cuts an element's hit region to the last source rectangle drawn unless preserveElementGeometry: true is honoured. The live check above reports what this browser does on the un-redrawn top half of a card.
    • 
    
  • Plain offscreen 2D caches trade memory for zero replay on clean cards. Holding 24 card-sized offscreen canvases roughly doubles canvas memory on the board (still under 12 MB on a phone at 2.625 scale), and needs window.__gfx.retire() on teardown so removed buffers don't linger until garbage collection.
