Parts of the native media player in a canvas

Chrome builds the controls of <video> and <audio> as elements in a shadow tree that pages cannot open. This page tests what a canvas can still do with them: restyle the parts, let the player's state drive a shader, and draw the controls apart from the picture.

How it works

  • The controls cannot be reached as elements. They can be reached with CSS: about twenty ::-webkit-media-controls-* pseudo-elements, and ::cue for captions. A canvas draws whatever those rules produce.
  • The state of the player is visible to CSS through :paused, :seeking, :muted and others where the browser has them. :has() carries that state to a parent, and a registered custom property carries it into a shader.
  • A canvas can get a video frame two ways: as part of the element's picture, with controls and captions, or bare with drawImage(video). Part 3 uses the bare frame for the picture and reduces the element to its controls.

Findings

From Chromium 157. The rows above say what your browser does.

  • Works: pseudo-element rules are drawn. Background, colour, accent-color, border-radius, display: none and zoom on ::-webkit-media-controls-* parts all show in the canvas, on <video> and <audio>, and the restyled controls keep working.
  • Works behind a flag: state pseudo-classes. :paused, :playing, :seeking, :muted, :buffering, :stalled and :volume-locked are not valid selectors in Chromium 157 by default. chromestatus lists them for Chrome 158. In 157 with experimental web platform features on they work, and :has(video:paused) on a canvas child changes a custom property that the shader reads. :picture-in-picture and :fullscreen work without the flag. Without the pseudo-classes, event listeners that set three attributes give the same result.
  • Works: HTML controls with no state code. In part 3 the labels of the HTML play and mute buttons and the busy ring are CSS content and display rules under :has(#lv:paused), :has(#lv:muted) and :has(#lv:buffering). The style change alone raises paint. Position and time still need script: CSS has no value for currentTime.
  • Works: the controls as their own layer. object-position: -9999px 0 moves the frame out of the element's box and leaves the controls on a clear background. An 80 px tall <video> is then a control strip. The canvas draws the frame from drawImage(video) with any filter, and draws the strip anywhere, at any size. Hit testing follows the strip.
  • Limit: no script access. video.shadowRoot is null and attachShadow() throws NotSupportedError. One part cannot be given drawable, measured, or drawn by itself. The page cannot ask where the play button is.
  • Limit: nothing can be put inside. Children of <video> are not rendered, so a canvas or any HTML cannot sit among the controls. HTML over the video is a sibling in the same drawn element (see HTML overlays on video).
  • Limit: the controls stay inside the element's box. A transform on the panel that moves it below the video is clipped. To put controls outside the picture, shrink the element to the controls (part 3) instead of moving the controls.
  • Limit: the buttons of a video fade during playback. After about three seconds of playback with no pointer over the video, Chrome fades the controls and then sets the panel to display: none. opacity: 1 !important on ::-webkit-media-controls-panel and ::-webkit-media-controls-timeline keeps the timeline. The row of buttons is ::-internal-media-controls-button-panel, a name pages cannot use, so no rule keeps it. It comes back when the pointer moves over the strip or the strip is tapped.
  • Works: an audio element as a control strip that never fades. The controls of <audio> stay visible. An audio element playing the same file, with a muted video that copies its play, pause, seek and speed, gives native controls under a picture the canvas draws. It has no fullscreen or captions button, and the two decoders need a drift correction.
  • Limit: one element, one hit region. The control strip can be moved and scaled as a whole. Its buttons cannot be placed one by one.
  • Native against HTML controls. Native controls bring the menu, captions switch, playback speed, Picture-in-Picture, keyboard and accessibility for no code, and can only be restyled part by part. Controls written in HTML take about 25 lines for play, seek, time and mute, can be laid out and drawn piece by piece, and have none of the rest until it is written.
  • Not verified here: which state pseudo-classes Chrome 159 on Android has by default, and how the touch layout of the controls (larger buttons, double-tap to seek) fits an 80 px strip.
